## Module 5: The business

**5(a) One sentence: how does it make money?**
Evercore sells advice to boards, companies, sponsors, creditors and private-fund managers on M&A, restructuring and liability management, secondaries, fundraising and capital raising. It is paid mostly through success fees when a transaction closes. Smaller revenue lines are underwriting spreads, agency equity commissions and research fees (Evercore ISI), and asset-based wealth-management fees. Its main cost is people: about 64% of net revenue goes to compensation.
Source: 10-K FY2025 Item 1 and MD&A "Revenue" (advisory fees are "dependent on the successful completion of client transactions"); FY2025 Adj. comp ratio of 64.2% from the 8-K of 4-Feb-2026.
Confidence: High.

**5(d) Position in the advisory value chain; revenue per SMD**
- **Independent, no balance sheet.** Evercore offers no lending, acquisition financing or proprietary trading. It competes against bulge brackets (GS, MS, JPM, BofA, Citi, Barclays, DB, UBS) and independents (Centerview, HLI, Lazard, Moelis, PWP, PJT, Rothschild) on conflict-free advice. This is the 10-K's own positioning.
- **Deal-size band.** Evercore is skewed to large-cap mandates: it advised on 5 of the top 15 global announced deals in 2025, including Warner Bros. Discovery (Netflix, then Paramount Skydance), Devon/Coterra ($58bn) and Axalta/AkzoNobel. The average fee per fee-earning advisory/underwriting transaction is about $4.3mm (2025: $3,447mm / 806 fees), versus $3.5mm in 2024 and $3.5–3.7mm in 2021–22. That is well above Houlihan Lokey's mid-market model.
- **Corporate vs sponsor.** Coverage is corporate-led. Sponsor work comes through the Financial Sponsors Group, PCA (GP-led continuation vehicles) and PFG. Sponsor revenue share is not disclosed (5e).
- **Revenue per SMD (est.)**, the analogue of "content per MW": advisory fees divided by the average of opening and closing IB/Advisory SMDs. Counts include committed hires, which biases the ratio downward.

**6(c) ROE / ROTCE vs cost of equity; returns on hiring and acquisitions**

FY2025 returns:
- Adj. net income $646.3mm on average Evercore Inc. stockholders' equity of $1,869.6mm gives an **Adj. ROE of about 34.6%**. On GAAP net income of $591.9mm, ROE is about 31.7%.
- Average tangible equity excludes goodwill of $230.8mm and intangibles of $30.1mm at end-2025, and goodwill of $124.5mm at end-2024. That gives about $1,677mm, so **Adj. ROTCE is about 38.5%**.
- Trough year 2023 (Adj. EPS $6.46): ROE was about 18–20% (est.; equity of about $1.6bn).
- Cost of equity is about 10–11% (est.: beta of about 1.3–1.4 × 5.5% equity risk premium + 10-year yield of about 4.5–5.3%).

ROE has exceeded the cost of equity in every year except 2008. That spread is the core of the quality case. ROE is flattered by a deliberately thin equity base: about $800mm a year is returned through dividends and buybacks.

Incremental returns:
1. **Lateral hiring.** Evercore added 41 external SMDs and promoted 25 over 2023–2025. The CFO says partner hires show good "NPVs and IRRs." A rough check (est.):
   - A lateral SMD costs about $4–6mm a year in guarantees and support for 2–3 years before ramping.
   - At the 2025 average of $21mm in revenue per SMD and an incremental operating margin of about 30%, a ramped SMD earns about $6mm a year.
   - So the payback is about 3–4 years in a mid-cycle market. That is acceptable, but cycle-dependent.
2. **Acquisitions.** ISI (2014) built a top-ranked research and ECM franchise. Commissions were $243mm in 2025, ECM grew, and Evercore ISI was Extel's #1 independent. Robey Warshaw (closed 1-Oct-2025) added 5 SMDs and UK board-level coverage. Goodwill rose by about $106mm. Consideration was £71.25mm in shares, of which £62.7mm is clawback-protected as comp, plus contingent shares on a 5-year benchmark. It is too early to measure returns. EMEA advisory had a record H1 2026.

Source: 10-K FY2025 balance sheet and Note 18; Q4 2025 call summary (41 external / 25 promoted); Q1 2026 call (NPV/IRR comment); FY2025 8-K.
Confidence: Medium-High for ROE/ROTCE; Low for per-SMD payback (est.).

**6(e) Is the current margin structural or cyclical?**

Mostly cyclical, with a structurally higher comp floor.

Evidence for cyclical:
1. The 2025/H1 2026 margin of 21.6–22.7% coincides with LSEG global announced M&A of about $4.5tn in 2025 (second-highest ever) and $3.9tn in 9M 2026 (highest for the period since 2001).
2. Q1 2026 was flattered by pull-forward.
3. Q2 2026 margin was 19.0%, with non-comp at 17.5%.
4. 2019 and 2022–23 show margins can fall 4–19 pp within 1–2 years.

Evidence for structural improvement:
1. The breadth of non-M&A revenue (about 45%) is higher than in 2019. PCA, PFG and equities had records.
2. Five straight years of buybacks exceeding RSU grants keep per-share economics intact.
3. The non-comp ratio is still below 2019's 17.3%.

Evidence for a structurally *higher* comp floor:
1. Comp ratios of 64–68% in 2023–2026 versus 56–59% in 2017–2021.
2. The CFO says "we're still a ways from sub 60" and 2026 improvement will be "meaningfully more modest."
3. Laterals are "more expensive" (CEO, Q4 2025).

A sensible mid-cycle Adj. operating margin is **about 18–20% (est.)**: the 2017–2025 average of 24.3% less the comp-ratio reset of about 5 pp. It is not the 22–25% of peak quarters.
Source: Table B-0; Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 call summaries; Investment Executive/LSEG (2-Oct-2026); Reuters/LSEG (4-Oct-2026).
Confidence: Medium-High.

**Key judgment (Module 6):** High returns on equity in every cycle except 2008. But operating leverage is strongly asymmetric (decrementals of 60–100%+, incrementals of about 30%) and the comp ratio has reset about 5–8 pp higher than last cycle. Current margins are near-peak and cyclical. Direction: **uncontrollable** (margins track the deal cycle; comp-ratio discipline is only partially controllable).

## Module 7: Growth

**7(a) Pipeline and backlog**

What Evercore discloses:
1. Qualitative backlog commentary: "at or near record" (Q4 2025), "strong and replenishing at a healthy rate" (Q1 2026), and "near record levels" plus strong engagement letters and conflict checks (Q2 2026, 29-Jul-2026).
2. Fee counts. Total fees were 296 in Q2 2026 (+21%) and 494 in H1 (+28%). Fees ≥$1mm were 132 in Q2 (+19%) and 280 in H1 (+36%).
3. The number of underwriting transactions: 26 in Q2 versus 13.
4. Notable announced mandates, which are leading indicators because fees usually close 3–9 months after announcement, and longer with regulatory review.

Evercore discloses no backlog dollars. Announced mega-mandates now in the conversion queue include Warner Bros. Discovery/Paramount Skydance ($110bn), Devon/Coterra ($58bn), Jetro/Sysco ($29.1bn), Beazley/Zurich (£8.2bn), Arcosa/CRH ($8.5bn) and Iridium/Rocket Lab ($8.0bn). **13(b) Switching costs**
Switching costs are moderate. Clients can hire any adviser for each deal, and there is no contract lock-in. Stickiness comes from:
1. Board trust and history. Special committees and defense work tend to rehire.
2. Banker-level relationships, which follow the banker. That is the risk.
3. Sponsor relationships in PCA and PFG, with repeat continuation-vehicle work.

Evercore's moat is therefore partly owned by its people. Deferred equity is the firm's way of turning personal relationships into institutional ones.
Source: 10-K FY2025 Risk Factors (dependence on senior professionals); Q2 2026 8-K (unvested RSUs).
Confidence: Medium.

## Module 15: Policy and regulation

**15(a) Antitrust and merger-review regime**
- **U.S.** The second Trump administration restored HSR early termination (Feb-2025) and re-embraced structural divestiture settlements. The FTC is now "more willing to constructively engage on remedies," per its May-2026 workshop, while still disfavoring behavioral remedies and late-arriving fixes. The new HSR form (effective Feb-2025) adds filing burden but not outcome risk.
- **Effect on EVR.** Higher completion probability for large strategic mergers, which was a 2025–26 tailwind. Timing risk remains for mega-mandates under review (WBD/Paramount Skydance, Devon/Coterra, Jetro/Sysco).
- **UK and EU.** The UK CMA has shifted to a pro-growth posture (2025), which supports EVR's expanded London franchise. The EU remains more interventionist on large tech and telecom deals.
- **Risk.** A policy reversal after the 2026 U.S. midterms or 2028 would lengthen timelines.

Source: Law-firm summaries of the FTC remedies workshop (JD Supra, May-2026); Freshfields and American Action Forum (FY2025 HSR report, 2-Jul-2026); 10-K FY2025 MD&A (regulatory approvals as a cause of failed or delayed deals).
Confidence: Medium.

**15(b) Rates, Fed policy and credit conditions**
- The Fed **raised** rates 25 bp to 3.75–4.00% on 16-Sep-2026 (12–0 vote; "Inflation remains elevated"). It was the first hike in over three years.
- The 10-year Treasury reached 5.34% (highest since 2002).
- Higher funding costs reduce LBO capacity, sponsor exits and valuations. That is negative for sponsor M&A, PFG fundraising and ECM in 2027.
- Higher-for-longer rates combined with the private-credit maturity wall support LM&R with a 2–4 quarter lag.
- Net: rates have turned from a tailwind (2024–25 cuts) into a **cap** on the cycle.

Source: FOMC statement, federalreserve.gov (monetary20260916a); Reuters/LSEG (4-Oct-2026).
Confidence: High for policy facts; Medium for the effect.

**15(c) Bankruptcy law and LME litigation**
- Courts have scrutinized non-pro-rata uptiers (Serta, 5th Cir. 2024–25; Mitel). The market responded by refining structures rather than abandoning LMEs: 47 in 2025, 37 with uptier elements.
- Europe is "meaningfully closing the gap" on LME usage.
- **For EVR:** more complex creditor-on-creditor situations mean more advisory mandates (cooperation agreements, multiple ad hoc groups) and longer engagements. It is net positive for LM&R fee pools, but fees in court are subject to approval and the U.S. Trustee's review.
- Third-party release limits after the Purdue ruling (2024) complicate mass-tort Chapter 11s but are minor for EVR.

Source: Ropes & Gray distressed-debt insights (Feb-2026); 10-K FY2025 MD&A (court approval of fees).
Confidence: Medium.

**15(d) Tax policy, SBC deductibility, non-competes**
- **Q1 vesting tax benefit.** Share-price appreciation on vesting RSUs reduced Q1 2026 tax by $88.5mm GAAP and $94.0mm Adj. That cut the Q1 effective tax rate to 2.7%/3.0% and the H1 Adj. rate to 12.1%. The benefit depends on the share price: at $272 versus 2026 vest-date prices around $320–345, the Q1 2027 benefit will be much smaller. That is a 2027 EPS headwind (est., magnitude to be quantified in Finance).
- **162(m).** The ARPA 2021 expansion of the $1mm deduction cap to the next five highest-paid employees takes effect for tax years beginning after 31-Dec-2026. That is a modest 2027+ tax-rate headwind for a firm with many highly paid SMDs (est.; to verify magnitude).
- **Corporate rate.** Unchanged at 21%; the 2025 tax act preserved it.
- **Carried interest.** Indirect relevance only, through sponsor economics; no change enacted (to verify).
- **Non-competes.** The FTC's 2024 non-compete ban was set aside by the courts and the FTC dropped its appeal (2025). Garden-leave and deferred-comp forfeiture remain enforceable, which preserves EVR's retention tools. UK reform proposals capping non-competes (3 months) would weaken London retention if enacted (to verify).

Source: Q1 2026 8-K (tax benefit); Q2 2026 8-K (effective tax rates); statutory knowledge (ARPA §9708, to verify); public record on the FTC non-compete rule (to verify).
Confidence: High for the Q1 tax benefit; Medium-Low for 162(m) and non-compete items.

**15(e) Financial regulation of broker-dealers and conflicts rules**
- Evercore Group L.L.C. is an SEC/FINRA broker-dealer subject to Rule 15c3-1 (net capital) and the Market Access Rule. UK entities are FCA-authorized under IFPR: minimum capital is the greater of £75k or one-quarter of fixed overheads. Evercore GmbH handles EU access after Brexit. Evercore Trust Company is OCC-regulated. EWM is an SEC-registered investment adviser.
- **Capital requirements are light** because the firm has no balance sheet.
- **Conflicts rules:** FINRA 2241 (research), placement-agent restrictions on public pensions (relevant to PFG/PCA), and the SEC's private-fund adviser rules, which were vacated in 2024. Continuation-vehicle fairness opinions are a growing area of scrutiny for PCA.
- **Net:** a low regulatory burden versus banks. Independence from Basel capital rules is a structural advantage.

Source: 10-K FY2025 Item 1 "Regulation."
Confidence: High.

**Key judgment (Module 15):** Antitrust policy is a current tailwind that could reverse. Monetary policy has just turned into a headwind with the Sept-2026 hike and a 5.3% 10-year. Tax tailwinds (the Q1 vesting benefit) shrink with a lower share price. Broker-dealer regulation is light. Direction: **uncontrollable**, deteriorating at the margin.
