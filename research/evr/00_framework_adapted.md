# Evercore (EVR) — 21-Module Framework, Adapted for an Advisory Investment Bank

Source framework: the user's "datacenter-infra-21-module-research" skill. Evercore is not a data-center
infrastructure company, so the user chose to keep the structure (3 independent sub-agents, cross-validation,
bias check, cycle-aware valuation, pre-mortem, Kelly sizing with a shared-factor cap) and replace the
data-center sub-questions with advisory equivalents. Every sub-question below is mandatory; numbering is fixed.

## What drives value here (replaces the four data-center drivers)
1. **Deal-cycle duration** — where the M&A / capital-markets / restructuring fee pool is in its cycle, and what
   Evercore's pipeline commentary and leading indicators say about it.
2. **Talent productivity and comp** — whether revenue per Senior Managing Director (SMD) and the comp ratio
   hold as the firm hires aggressively; comp is the "capacity cost" of this business.
3. **Franchise and share** — Evercore's share of the global advisory fee pool, its mix (strategic M&A,
   restructuring/liability management, private capital advisory, equities, wealth), and whether structural
   shifts (private credit, sponsor in-housing, AI) add or remove fee pool.
4. **Cycle-adjusted valuation** — what growth and margin the current price implies (reverse DCF / implied
   growth on mid-cycle EPS), compared with a mid-cycle view.

**Portfolio fact:** advisory boutiques are largely **one bet on the deal cycle** (M&A volumes, sponsor
activity, equity markets, credit spreads). Sizing is capped by aggregate exposure to that factor (21b) —
including other holdings that move with it (other boutiques, alt managers, brokers, cyclicals).

## Data conventions
- Evercore reports U.S. GAAP and "Adjusted" (non-GAAP) results. Consensus is on Adjusted. Never mix them in
  one table without labeling. Fiscal year = calendar year.
- Valuation metrics: P/E (NTM, Adjusted), P/E on mid-cycle EPS, market cap, net cash. EV/EBITDA is not the
  standard metric for an advisory firm; use Adjusted operating income where an EV-based multiple is needed.
- Not a recent IPO (IPO Aug-2006); full history applies, including the 2008–09, 2019–20 and 2023 downturns.

## People (Modules 1–4) → Appendix A

**Module 1: Management**
- a) CEO(s), Chairman and CFO: background, tenure, how they came to the role; the co-CEO / leadership transitions since 2020
- b) Capital allocation track record: acquisitions (ISI 2014, Robey Warshaw 2025, others) — price paid and outcome; buybacks; dividend; lateral hiring as the main "capex"
- c) Compensation design from the DEF 14A: metrics used and what behavior they reward; deferred comp structure for SMDs
- d) Credibility: Evercore gives no numerical guidance — compare management's stated outlooks (comp-ratio intentions, pipeline/backlog commentary, margin goals) with outcomes over the last 8+ quarters
- e) Bench depth and succession; key-person and key-banker risk (revenue concentration in top rainmakers)

**Module 2: Governance**
- a) Board independence, relevant experience, staggered board, controlled-company status
- b) Related-party arrangements
- c) Insider selling and buying (Form 4), 10b5-1 usage, size relative to holdings
- d) Shareholder rights: voting structure (Class A / Class B), takeover defenses
- e) Pay-for-performance alignment; dilution from equity comp (stock comp as % of revenue; share count)

**Module 3: Ownership & overhang**
- a) Insider and employee ownership, float; legacy partnership structure (Evercore LP units exchangeable into Class A shares)
- b) Overhang: remaining exchangeable LP units, unvested equity awards and their vesting schedule (replaces lockups)
- c) Tax receivable agreement (TRA) with former partners: size and remaining payments
- d) Institutional concentration (13F), short interest, days to cover
- e) Share count trend: SBC dilution vs. buybacks, over 10 years

**Module 4: Ground-level substitutes**
- a) Employee signals: Glassdoor / Wall Street Oasis commentary, banker departures and arrivals (SMD net adds), junior hiring
- b) Client signals: league-table ranks (announced and completed M&A, restructuring), notable mandates won/lost, fee-paying client counts
- c) Talent-market constraints: lateral hiring competition, guarantees, non-competes, poaching of Evercore teams
- d) Controversies: regulatory actions (FINRA/SEC/FCA), litigation (e.g., fairness-opinion suits, liability-management litigation), conflicts
- e) Culture and training: analyst/associate programs, promotion vs. lateral mix, ESG ratings and weak spots

People report ends with: **Strengths ⭐ / Risks ⚠️ (≥5 each)** + **information-gap table**.

## Business (Modules 5–7, 9–10, 12–13, 15) → Appendix B

**Module 5: The business**
- a) One sentence: how does it make money?
- b) Segment mix: Investment Banking & Equities vs. Investment Management (wealth); within IB: advisory, underwriting, commissions & related, other — revenue and share, last two periods
- c) **Fee-pool exposure** (replaces DC exposure): % of revenue from strategic M&A, restructuring / liability management, private capital advisory & private funds group, equity capital markets, equities, wealth — disclosed or estimated, with method
- d) Position in the advisory value chain: independent advice vs. balance-sheet banks; deal-size bands; corporate vs. sponsor coverage; **revenue per SMD** (est.) as the analogue of content per MW
- e) Client structure: corporate vs. sponsor; top-client concentration; geography (US vs. EMEA vs. other)

**Module 6: Business quality**
- a) Revenue and EPS CAGR across full cycles (include 2008–09, 2019–20, 2023 downturns)
- b) Margin drivers decomposed: comp ratio, non-comp ratio, revenue leverage (pp contribution each)
- c) ROE / ROTCE vs. cost of equity; incremental returns on lateral hiring and acquisitions
- d) Operating leverage: incremental margins in upcycles, **decremental margins in past downturns**
- e) Is the current margin structural or cyclical? Evidence either way

**Module 7: Growth**
- a) Pipeline and backlog: what Evercore discloses (qualitative backlog commentary, fee-paying clients, deals >$1M fees), announced-deal leading indicators, conversion lag from announcement to fee
- b) Capacity: SMD headcount, hiring/promotion pace, ramp time, revenue per SMD trend (nominal and real)
- c) Driver map with certainty (high/medium/low): M&A volumes, sponsor activity, rates and credit spreads, maturity wall / restructuring, ECM, equity markets
- d) S-curve position of each business line (strategic M&A, LM&R, PCA/PFG, ECM, equities/ISI, wealth, international)
- e) TAM build: global advisory fee pool × Evercore share, and the share assumption
- f) Cycle assumption: the deal-cycle stage this report assumes (early / mid / late / turning), evidence, and the 2–3 signals that would change it

**Module 9: Structural shifts & cost inputs**
- a) Structural shifts — private credit replacing syndicated/bankruptcy processes, sponsor in-house M&A teams, AI in banking (junior productivity, disintermediation), bulge-bracket competition for independent advice: beneficiary, neutral or displaced? Timeline
- b) Main input cost = talent: comp inflation, guarantees for laterals, deferred comp amortization
- c) Pass-through: fee rates and pricing power on fees; retainer vs. success fee mix
- d) Bottlenecks: senior-talent supply, regulatory approval timelines for deals (which delay fee recognition)
- e) Recurring / less cyclical revenue: wealth management, equities commissions, retainers, restructuring as counter-cyclical hedge — share and margin

**Module 10: Competitive landscape**
- a) Bargaining power: talent (suppliers) / firm / clients (corporates, sponsors)
- b) Peer table: PJT, Lazard, Moelis, Houlihan Lokey, Perella Weinberg, plus bulge brackets (GS, MS, JPM) for advisory share — revenue growth, comp ratio, op margin, revenue per MD, core weakness
- c) Sources of advantage
- d) Concentration and consolidation (boutique acquisitions, e.g., Greenhill by Mizuho; Evercore–Robey Warshaw)
- e) Supply response: new boutique formation, poaching, how quickly rivals add senior bankers; signs fee competition is intensifying

**Module 12: Franchise & platform** (replaces technology moat)
- a) Product roadmap: new businesses (PCA, LM&R build-out, international, ECM) and time to scale
- b) Relationship position with CEOs/boards and sponsors; repeat-client share
- c) Integrated-solution strategy (advisory + capital markets + equities research): margin and conflict risk
- d) Scale: global footprint, sector coverage breadth, research platform (ISI)
- e) How many years before a rival could replicate the franchise — with reasoning

**Module 13: Moat**
- a) Pricing power: fee levels through past downturns, not just now
- b) Switching costs: relationships, board trust, repeat mandates
- c) Scale economics: brand, league tables, research, global coverage
- d) Reputation, regulatory standing; dispute history
- e) Premium vs. peers (revenue per MD, margin, valuation), quantified

**Module 15: Policy & regulation**
- a) Antitrust/merger-review regime (US FTC/DOJ, EU, UK CMA) and its effect on deal volume and timing
- b) Rates, Fed policy and credit conditions as a cap/driver on sponsor activity
- c) Bankruptcy law and liability-management litigation (effect on restructuring fees)
- d) Tax policy (corporate rates, carried interest, deductibility of SBC — EVR's Q1 vesting tax benefit), non-compete regulation
- e) Financial regulation of broker-dealers (FINRA, SEC, FCA); conflicts rules

Business report ends with: **Scorecard** (each module 1–10 + trend arrow) + **information-gap table (≥5 items)**.

## Finance (Modules 8, 11, 14, 17–21) → Appendix C

**Module 8: Revenue quality**
- a) Growth split: organic (SMD capacity × productivity) / acquisitions / FX
- b) Seasonality and lumpiness (Q4 heavy; deal timing)
- c) Revenue recognition: success fees at completion vs. retainers; contract assets; reimbursable expenses gross-up
- d) Concentration: largest client fees and number of large-fee clients
- e) Geographic mix and FX

**Module 11: Profitability**
- a) Comp ratio, non-comp ratio, operating margin history vs. peers (GAAP and Adjusted, labeled)
- b) Revenue sensitivity: ±10% revenue → ±EPS, given sticky comp (empirical decremental margin)
- c) Comp-ratio trend vs. revenue-per-SMD trend
- d) Segment margins (IB & Equities vs. Investment Management)
- e) **Mid-cycle margin estimate** and its basis

**Module 14: Pre-mortem**
At least three ways to die. **Two are mandatory:** (1) the deal cycle turns or stalls; (2) margins mean-revert — comp ratio rises as hiring outruns revenue and the talent war resumes. Third and beyond are company-specific (key-banker defections, restructuring share loss, private credit / AI disintermediation, failed acquisition integration).
Each: **Scenario / Trigger / Current status & response / Probability / Impact (revenue, EPS, multiple, value per share) / Source & confidence.**
End with a **bear-case summary table including the compound scenario**, and answer: *Is the real risk an earnings collapse or multiple compression from being re-rated as peak-cycle?*

**Module 17: Balance sheet**
- a) Cash and investments vs. debt (senior notes), net cash; accrued comp (year-end bonuses) as an offset to cash
- b) Debt maturity ladder, fixed vs. floating
- c) Working capital: receivables and contract assets vs. accrued comp
- d) Goodwill and intangibles (ISI, other acquisitions); impairment history
- e) Off-balance-sheet / other: TRA, leases, deferred comp obligations, guarantees, litigation, regulatory capital
- f) Capex: office build-outs, technology; minimal growth capex — hiring is the real investment

**Module 18: Accounting quality**
- a) Auditor, opinion, any material weakness or auditor change
- b) Non-GAAP: Adjusted vs. GAAP gap, add-backs (acquisition-related comp, special charges, LP-unit treatment), trend
- c) Receivables days and contract assets vs. revenue growth
- d) Deferred comp accounting (amortization timing), accrual estimates, Q1 share-vesting tax benefit and its recurrence
- e) Restatements and SEC comment letters

**Module 19: Cash flow**
- a) Operating CF / net income across years (bonus seasonality)
- b) FCF and FCF yield
- c) Is FCF funding hiring plus capital return?
- d) Dividend coverage
- e) Buybacks net of SBC dilution (share count over time)

**Module 20: Valuation**
- a) P/E (Adjusted, NTM and TTM) vs. own history — **split pre-2020 vs. current cycle**
- b) Peer comps table (P/E NTM, P/E on trailing, dividend + buyback yield, growth, comp ratio, margin)
- c) **Reverse DCF / implied growth:** growth, margin and duration implied by the current price vs. base case
- d) Scenario DCF including a mid-cycle-margin case
- e) N/A (not a recent IPO) — state so

**Module 21: Decision & sizing**
- a) Three scenarios: FY+2 Adjusted EPS × target P/E → value per share vs. current (no SOTP unless justified)
- b) Sizing: Kelly f* = (p×b − q)/b, where **b uses the bear-case value from Module 14**; p stated with basis; ×0.5 safety factor; then **cap by deal-cycle factor exposure** (position + all holdings that move with M&A/capital-markets activity within a stated basket limit). Report the binding constraint.
- c) Buy zones (six tiers: deeply undervalued → overvalued) in P/E NTM and P/E on mid-cycle EPS, with prices; exit conditions in four categories with numeric thresholds (fundamentals, e.g. comp ratio up YoY and advisory revenue down YoY for two quarters, or SMD net departures; valuation; industry turn, e.g. announced M&A volume down >20% YoY for two quarters; discipline stop-loss)
- d) Catalysts and headwinds with timing (Q3'26 earnings 28-Oct-2026, Fed decisions, maturity wall, investor day)
- e) One-sentence thesis

Finance report ends with: **data appendix** (all available years of core financials + valuation snapshot).

## Module 16: Bias check (main agent only)
Nine items: 1 Confirmation 2 Recency (extrapolating current-cycle margins) 3 Narrative hindsight 4 Anchoring
(to peak price, prior thesis, or consensus) 5 Over-optimism 6 Neglect of competition / talent supply response
7 Survivorship 8 Groupthink 9 **Theme crowding** (owning the deal-cycle trade through many correlated names).
End with a before/after valuation table (EPS, target multiple, mid-cycle margin, base value).

## Formatting (all agents)
Every sub-question ends with `Source:` and `Confidence:` (High 80%+ / Medium-High 65–80% / Medium 50–65% /
Low <50%). Estimates marked "(est.)". $mm or $bn consistently; ratios to one decimal. Tables for comparisons.
Each module ends with "Key judgment:" plus a directional word (rising / falling / controllable /
uncontrollable / leading / lagging). No pasted sell-side or news text; no unsourced "insiders say"; no target
price presented as certain.
