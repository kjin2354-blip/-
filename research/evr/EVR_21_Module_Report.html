Exit conditions

  • Fundamentals: comp ratio up year over year and advisory revenue down year over year for 2 straight quarters; 5+ net SMD departures in 12 months or loss of a named top producer; trailing operating margin below 17%.

  • Industry turn: announced global M&A down >20% year over year for 2 quarters; high-yield spreads >500bp; 10-year >5.75% for a month.
  • Stop-loss: halve at −20% from cost, exit at −30%, and re-underwrite after any quarter that misses revenue by >15%.

9Thesis

Evercore is the best-diversified independent advisory franchise: 188 senior bankers, ~35% ROE, net cash of $1.9bn, and a business mix in which restructuring and private capital cushion, but do not offset, an M&A downturn. At $271.86 it looks cheap on this cycle's earnings (14.3x trailing, ~12.8x forward) and expensive on an average year's (~20.5x on $13–13.5 mid-cycle EPS). The price needs ~6.4% perpetual growth in mid-cycle earnings at an 11.5% cost of equity, or a decade of compounding near its historical trend. The moat is real but shows up as share, not margin, and revenue per banker has been flat in real terms for a decade, so growth depends on hiring that keeps comp around 64%. Downside support is the net cash (~$21/share after bonuses) and a trough-cycle valuation near $165. The next catalyst is Q3 results on 28 Oct. The biggest risk is an earnings collapse, not a re-rating: past downturns took 60–100%+ of lost revenue out of operating income. The deal market is softening into a 5.3% 10-year, and that has already started.
