### 1c) Compensation design (DEF 14A): metrics used, behavior rewarded, SMD deferral structure

- **No formula.** NEO bonuses are fully discretionary: "no … compensation pursuant to formulaic or other pre-established performance targets". For pay-versus-performance (SEC-required) purposes, the Committee lists Adjusted Net Revenues, Adjusted EPS, Adjusted Net Income and Adjusted Operating Margin. All four are **non-GAAP**. None measures return on capital, TSR relative to peers, or comp ratio.
- **Cap mechanism:** U.S. NEO cash bonuses are paid as distributions on Evercore LP **Class L Interests**. These are capped at a share of 2025 Adjusted Operating Income: 2.00% each for Altman and Weinberg (≈$16.8mm cap each on $839mm Adj. op income, est.), 1.25% for LaLonde, 1.00% for Klurfeld. The structure also helps Section 162(m) tax efficiency.
- **Deferral:** 50% of U.S. NEO bonuses and 60% for the UK NEO (regulatory requirement) are paid in RSUs vesting over 4 years. Broad SMD awards follow the same pattern. Deferred share rises with seniority and bonus size.
  - Retirement vesting: awards keep vesting after retirement (age 55 + 5 years' service with age+service > 65, or age 60 + 10 years) **only if the retiree complies with non-competes**. In effect this is a non-compete of up to 4 years.
  - RSUs accelerate on change in control and on termination without cause.
- **Long-term plans for IB SMDs (not NEOs):** the 2021 and 2025 LTIPs pay for beating "defined benchmark results" over 4 years. $164.1mm was accrued at YE25, and $240.5mm of future expense remains for the 2025 LTIP. Class K-P performance units add to this (see 3b).
- **Ownership guidelines:** 200k shares for the CEO and Senior Chairman. Other SMDs: the lesser of 40k shares or 50% of 4 years of bonus RSUs.

### 1e) Bench depth, succession, key-person and key-banker risk

- **No named CEO successor.** The 2026 proxy assigns the Nominating & Governance Committee "succession planning for our *directors*". It does not describe an executive-succession process. The combined Chair/CEO is 69 and the founder 80. Weinberg is an at-will employee (his agreement expired Mar-2023). With one year's notice he is retirement-eligible with full continued vesting, so a departure would carry no financial penalty for him.
- **Bench:** the Management Committee includes LaLonde (64), Klurfeld (52) and Lindsey-Clark (63), plus business heads not named as NEOs. No internal heir has been made visible through the NEO group or a President/COO title. *Not disclosed / To verify:* names and ages of the U.S. Advisory heads.
- **Key-banker concentration:**
  - No client >10% of revenue (2023–25). Fees ≥$1mm: 378 → 457 → **529** (2023 → 2025). Total fees earned: 806 (2025).
  - Revenue per individual rainmaker is not disclosed.
  - Est. 2025 advisory fees ≈$3.3bn ÷ ~158 average IB SMDs ≈ **$21mm per IB SMD (est.)**. Top-decile bankers likely produce well above that.
  - Altman (80) and Weinberg (69) are explicitly credited with major client relationships.
  - Robey Warshaw adds concentrated UK relationships held by a few founders (Robey, Warshaw, Osborne). They are held in place only by the 4-year clawback on closing shares and the multi-year earn-out.
- **Mitigants:** a 4-year deferral cliff on RSUs; post-retirement vesting conditional on non-competes; 2025 LTIP accrual ($240mm future expense) paying out in 2029–31; equity ownership guidelines for all SMDs.

Source: DEF 14A 2026 (governance, executive officers, Weinberg agreement); 10-K FY2025 (Item 1, Note 18, client concentration, fees ≥$1mm table); Q4'25 release.
Confidence: Medium.

## Strengths ⭐

1. ⭐ **Delivered on comp-ratio discipline while hiring:** Adj. comp ratio 67.6% (FY23) → 63.8% (1H26) as IB SMDs grew 136 → 188.
2. ⭐ **Recruiting engine with low disclosed attrition:** est. ~4% gross SMD exits in 2025; 1–2 known departures per quarter.
3. ⭐ **Consistent anti-dilution buybacks:** 111% of net grants repurchased (2023–25). Adjusted diluted shares are down 16% from the 2015 peak.
4. ⭐ **No controlling shareholder:** annual board elections, 82% independent board, small TRA ($70mm, ≈0.6% of market cap), say-on-pay ~94%.
5. ⭐ **Deep deferral and clawback architecture:** 50–60% of NEO bonuses in 4-year RSUs; retirement vesting conditional on non-competes; RW sellers' shares are clawback-protected for 4 years.
6. ⭐ **Broad client base:** no client >10%; 529 fees ≥$1mm (+16% YoY); #3 global advisory revenue among public firms.
7. ⭐ **CEO is meaningfully invested:** Weinberg holds ≈$265mm of stock and units, with no discretionary sales in the past 12 months.

## Risks ⚠️

1. ⚠️ **Succession opacity:** no disclosed CEO succession plan. CEO is 69 and at-will, retirement-eligible on one year's notice; the founder-rainmaker is 80. No visible heir among the NEOs.
2. ⚠️ **Pay outran performance in 2025:** CEO +73% and Senior Chairman +133% vs Adj. EPS +55%. All metrics are discretionary and non-GAAP. Altman's 280G gross-up / $118mm CIC package is a legacy governance flaw.
3. ⚠️ **Dilution tolerance is wearing thin:** the equity plan passed with only 68% support; potential dilution is 28%. Buybacks run at $0.6–0.7bn a year, which is cyclically vulnerable.
4. ⚠️ **Rising cost of talent:** forgivable-loan comp +80% in 2 years; RW at ~$39mm per SMD; management calls recruiting more expensive. Non-comp grew +27% in 1H26 vs guidance of ~17%.
5. ⚠️ **Robey Warshaw execution and key-person risk:** a few founders hold the relationships. GAAP accretion is not yet visible. The ~$100mm second tranche was due ~1-Oct-2026.
6. ⚠️ **New UK/non-U.S. employment-tax exposure:** a $24.9mm liability booked; it could grow and raise ongoing EMEA comp cost.
7. ⚠️ **Aging, long-tenured board:** average age ≈73; lead director in her 20th year; limited technology/AI expertise.
8. ⚠️ **Buyback price insensitivity:** 1H26 buybacks averaged $324.60, ~19% above the current price.
