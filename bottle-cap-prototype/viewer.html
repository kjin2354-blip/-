Cross-Blade Powder Cap

These are the actual print files, not a concept render. Pressing the head drives an X-shaped blade through a foil disc. The slanted edges puncture the centre first and then cut outward, so the foil opens as four flaps that stay attached.

Assemble

  1. Stick a disc of aluminium foil tape, about 24 mm across, on the flat ring around the floor opening inside the chamber. Press the edges down firmly.
  2. Fill the chamber with powder.
  3. Push the plunger up through the lid from underneath, blade end down, then press the lid into the chamber.
  4. Press the head onto the peg. Add a drop of glue if it's loose.
  5. Drop the washer into the pocket under the cap, snap the safety clip around the shaft, and screw the cap onto a filled bottle.

Before you print

  1. Check the bottle is PCO 1881. The thread is set to the published PCO 1881 standard used on Dasani. A sealed cap should come off in about 1¾ turns. The thread root diameter (24.94 mm) is provisional, so check it with calipers if you can.
  2. Set CLEARANCE and LID_FIT for your printer at the top of cap.py. Resin defaults are set; for filament use about 0.35 and 0.15, and BLADE_T 1.2.
  3. Re-run python cap.py to regenerate the STL files.

What to test

  1. Press force: stand the bottle on a kitchen scale and press. Peak reading in kg × 10 ≈ newtons. Aim for under 2–3 kg.
  2. Opening: do all four flaps fold clear, and does all the powder fall through?
  3. Leaks: screw on, turn upside down, squeeze the bottle.

Known compromise: the cap is tall (74 mm) because 34 mL of powder space and a 14 mm stroke both add height. A smaller dose or a steeper blade shortens it.
